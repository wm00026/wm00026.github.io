# Taking Notes on Programming (and other topics in general)

***Date: 26 July 2026***

Taking notes is one of those skills for learning that I have, personally, struggled with at times. Making the notes themselves useful is something that I have tried to work on for a while.

This blog post discusses... I guess how I think about taking notes. What information I look for, how I take my notes, and how I've come to do so.

## How (and why) I Take My Notes
My note taking process is simple. 

For computerized notes, I use markdown as the base of my notes. To manage it, I use Obsidian, which acts like my "notes repo" in a way. This works best for classes for computers: data structures, computer systems, networking, things that include code or are better to type out rather than write down.

For "paper" notes, I use a tablet. Not that there is any terrible difference between digital paper notes and paper notes, I just find digital paper notes more convienient for transfer and it makes my handwriting a bit better. I find these notes are best for things with math or aren't much better with a computer.

For things that could be taken either way, usually I just use whatever's convinient. If there's a PowerPoint to reference, I'll use my computer. If its easier for desk-space or diagrams, I'll use paper. 

What doesn't change (really) no matter what interface I write my notes on is how I structure my notes.

```md
A Basic Structure:
*Date*
**Chapter or Section**
notes go here
    -> This is how I draw bulletpoints
    -> Lots of shorthand (w/, &, etc.)
```

## What Information To Look For
When I'm taking notes, I try to only write the information that *seems* important. It helps when there's a PowerPoint or material on the board, of course that makes it easy to determine what is important and what really isn't.

I *try* not to write down what the speaker is saying word for word. Now, I don't always shorten and synthesize what the speaker is saying in my own words. If its a definition, or a note, I'll probably write down what the speaker is saying.

## Studying My Notes
Studying my notes is also something else that I have struggled with. But I think I've come up with a system:

- I highlight sections and "pretty" up my notes so they are at least somewhat appealing and structured. If I don't look at the notes because I can't find information... they're kind of pointless.
- I may rewrite sections that feel important for an upcoming exam or quiz: This works best for math classes.
- There's always good old flashcards and making practice quizzes. It's really easy with typed notes to do that.

I know these are "basic" note tips... but I don't think most notes have to be complicated. As long as they are useful, and are actually helpful for studying, then they work. And for me, they work.
